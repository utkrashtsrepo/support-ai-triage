01 · The Problem

The hard part isn't reading a ticket.

It's deciding what the ticket actually means — and whether you're confident enough to act on it.

Customer ticket

“I cancelled my subscription last week, but I was charged again and I still haven't received my refund.”

02 · Core Insight

The product shouldn't just classify tickets. It should know when classification is safe.

This became the foundation of the human-in-the-loop experience.

03 · AI Experimentation

I didn't trust the first prompt.

I tested the model, inspected where it failed, changed the prompt based on those failures, and tested again.

V1 · unseen synthetic set
8/10

Financial keywords could overpower the actual context.

V2 · same unseen set
10/10

Targeted ambiguity and insufficient-evidence rules fixed the observed failures.

Failure A — Payment flow error

V1 leaned toward Billing because of payment language.

Context beats keywords
Failure B — “Money disappeared.”

V1 was confident despite insufficient evidence.

Confidence ≠ certainty

These are prototype results on small synthetic benchmarks, not production accuracy.

07 · Deliberate Scope

What I didn't build.

Autonomous routing

Human confirmation stays central.

Customer auto-replies

No unsupervised customer communication.

Generic chatbot

Focused on agent workflow.

CRM replacement

Copilot, not a new helpdesk.

RAG-heavy engine

Triage remains the core problem.

Large BI suite

Avoiding feature bloat.

08 · Evidence vs Validation

Still unvalidated
  • Real support-agent usability
  • Real-world ticket performance
  • Actual time-to-triage improvement
  • Long-term adoption and trust
Reflection

AI product design isn't about making the model look confident.

It's about designing what happens when the model is uncertain — and giving the human the right amount of control.
